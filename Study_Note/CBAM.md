### 1. What was CBAM designed to achieve?
CBAM (Convolutional Block Attention Module) was designed to boost the representational power of CNNs by explicitly teaching the network what features to emphasize and where in the image to focus, while remaining lightweight enough to plug into any existing CNN architecture (such as ResNet, VGG, or MobileNet) with negligible parameter and FLOP overhead.
### 2. What is the difference between channel attention and spatial attention?
Channel Attention ("What" is meaningful): Compresses spatial dimensions to score the importance of each feature channel. It determines which learned filters or sensor carry the most useful semantic information (for example, prioritizing vertical acceleration channels over noisy data during walking).
Spatial Attention ("Where" or "When" is meaningful): Compresses information across the channel axis to generate an attention map across spatial locations (in images) or time steps (in 1D sensor windows). It highlights where in the receptive field—or at which exact moments in a time-series window, such as a heel strike—the most discriminative signal occurs.
### 3. Why does CBAM use both global average pooling (GAP) and global max pooling (GMP) when computing channel attention?
Using only a single pooling operation (such as GAP alone in SENet) discards part of the feature distribution inside each channel. By computing both GAP and GMP in parallel and passing them through a shared Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) before summing their outputs, CBAM provides two complementary summaries of every channel, which create finer channel attention weights than either pooling method alone. In ablation experiments by the CBAM authors, combining GAP + GMP consistently outperformed GAP-only or GMP-only channel attention across image classification and object detection benchmarks.
### 4. What types of information do GAP and GMP capture, respectively?
Global Average Pooling (GAP): Captures overall global context and sustained activation—measuring how strongly and consistently a feature is present across the entire spatial area or time window.
Global Max Pooling (GMP): Captures localized responses—identifying the single strongest activation spike (such as a distinct object edge or a brief, sharp motion impact) even when that event occupies only a small fraction of the window.
### 5. Why is channel attention applied before spatial attention in CBAM?
Empirical ablation studies in the original CBAM paper showed that sequential Channel Spatial ordering outperforms both Spatial Channel and parallel arrangements. Channels first suppresses noisy or irrelevant feature maps so that the spatial attention layer aggregates cleaner, semantically meaningful features rather than being misled by background noise in uninformative channels.
### 6. What types of information can a network select more effectively with CBAM?
By combining channel and spatial attention sequentially, a network can simultaneously select:
Semantic / Modal Relevance ("What"): Which learned feature detectors or physical sensor channels (body_acc, body_gyro, total_acc) are most reliable for distinguishing the target class.
Spatial / Temporal Localization ("Where" / "When"): Which specific regions of an image—or which exact time steps within a continuous wearable sensor window—contain the key action while ignoring background clutter or stationary intervals.
